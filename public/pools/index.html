Office Pools

A river of free-to-play desk pools. Pick a coin, a series, a forecast, or a Noun. The prize is a plaque and a pixel ticket. Nobody loses anything, because nobody puts anything in.

made for the pump.fun floor, and for anyone who wants to keep score for fun. not a fund, not a prize pot, not yield.

the river

A trunk, then six forks

Office Pools is the main stream. Each fork is its own mini pool, with its own clock and its own board. Pick a branch.

Clock in

A desk name is enough to enter. A wallet address is optional and for display only. We never ask you to sign, send, or deposit.

prize and odds

Nobody loses anything

The shape is borrowed from PoolTogether, a no-loss prize savings protocol that used to work well: simple tickets, a clock everyone can see, a clear prize, published odds, a winners tape, and a board. The difference is the stake. They held a deposit and paid a prize from yield. We hold nothing.

The prize

Bragging rights, a name on the winners tape, and a pixel-Noun ticket you can screenshot. There is no pot, no yield, no token, and no cash. The plaque is the prize.

The ticket and the odds

One clock-in, one ticket, one pick per round. If your side wins, you are a winner with everyone who picked it. If K tickets picked the winning side, you share the plaque, and desk-captain odds on that side are 1 in K. If your side does not win, you still lose nothing.

The clock

Rounds close on a visible bell in Eastern Time. Pump Pools also run 15-minute and hourly heats. Entries freeze at the bell. This prototype keeps tickets on your device; a later worker could publish a shared tape.

                        field notes · November 15, 2026
                        
    
                        Field notes
                        
    
                        
      
                        Office Pools is a free-to-play scoreboard for the Office Noun desk. It is not a savings product, not a lottery you pay to enter, and not a fund. The honest line is the same one on the rest of the desk: made for fun, no promises.
                        
      
                        We looked at PoolTogether as a reference point of something that was working. From it we kept the parts that made the game legible: a ticket you can understand, a clock you can point at, a prize and an odds screen in plain language, a “nobody loses anything” promise, a winners tape, and a leaderboard. We did not keep the deposit, the yield, or the pot. Those are the parts that need a licensed product and a treasury. We did not build those parts, and we are not pretending to.
                        
      
                        The page is a river because the idea should be easy to fork. The trunk is Office Pools. The branches are Pump Pools, Series GvG, Desk League, Weather Pools, Noun Draw, and an empty bed for a desk that wants its own rules. Each branch has a clock and a board. The dry channel carries a JSON template so a new community can copy the rules without asking us for a pot.
                        
      
                        Design choices, briefly: the colors and the desk are the Office Noun ones, so this sits next to the market and the terminal without inventing a new brand. The type is IBM Plex Serif for reading, Sans for the interface, and Mono for clocks, tickets, and boards. The Nouns are official CC0 pixel art, drawn as bricks at integer scale so the edges stay crisp. Example data is labeled. Financial figures that we cannot see are shown as a dash, never invented.
                        
      
                        Rules, in short:
                        
      
                          
        
                        • Free to play. No entry fee, no pot, no yield, no deposits.
                          • 
        
                        • A desk name is enough. A wallet is optional and is a name tag, not a login.
                          • 
        
                        • One pick per person per round. You may change it until the bell.
                          • 
        
                        • Scoring uses public market data and a published formula. Missing prints are skipped, not zeroed.
                          • 
        
                        • All correct picks win the plaque. A desk captain may be drawn from them. Nobody loses a stake, because there is no stake.
                          • 
      
                        
      
                        A longer note, including what a backend would have to do, is in FIELD-NOTES.md and README.md.
